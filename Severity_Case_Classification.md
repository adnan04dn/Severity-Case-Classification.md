# Severity & Case Classification of Adverse Drug Reactions

## Introduction

Severity and seriousness are important concepts in pharmacovigilance. They help evaluate the clinical impact of adverse drug reactions (ADRs) and determine appropriate case handling and reporting.

Severity describes the intensity of an event, such as mild, moderate, or severe. Seriousness is a regulatory concept based on specific outcome or medical criteria and is not synonymous with severity.

## Severity Classification

### 1. Mild
The reaction causes minimal discomfort and usually does not interfere significantly with normal activities.

Example:
Mild headache after taking a medicine.

### 2. Moderate
The reaction causes noticeable discomfort or interferes with normal activities and may require medical treatment.

Example:
Moderate vomiting requiring treatment but not hospitalization.

### 3. Severe
The reaction is intense and may significantly affect the patient's condition or require urgent medical attention.

Example:
Severe bronchospasm requiring emergency treatment.

## Seriousness Classification

An adverse event is considered serious when it results in one or more of the following:

- Death
- Life-threatening condition
- Hospitalization or prolongation of hospitalization
- Persistent or significant disability/incapacity
- Congenital anomaly or birth defect
- Other medically important events requiring medical or surgical intervention to prevent a serious outcome

## Expectedness

An adverse reaction is considered expected when its nature and severity are consistent with the applicable reference safety information or product information.

An unexpected reaction is one whose nature, severity, or specificity is not consistent with the applicable reference information.

## Case Study 1 – Mild, Non-serious, Expected

A patient develops a mild headache after taking a medicine. The headache resolves without treatment and does not interfere significantly with daily activities. Headache is listed in the product information.

**Severity:** Mild

**Seriousness:** Non-serious

**Expectedness:** Expected

**Clinical impact:** Minimal; no hospitalization or significant intervention required.

## Case Study 2 – Moderate, Non-serious

A patient develops moderate nausea after starting a medicine. The patient requires an antiemetic but does not require hospitalization. Nausea is listed in the product information.

**Severity:** Moderate

**Seriousness:** Non-serious

**Expectedness:** Expected

**Clinical impact:** Requires treatment but does not meet serious-event criteria.

## Case Study 3 – Severe and Serious

A patient develops severe anaphylaxis after administration of a medicine. Emergency treatment and hospitalization are required.

**Severity:** Severe

**Seriousness:** Serious

**Expectedness:** Depends on the applicable product information.

**Clinical impact:** Significant; the event is life-threatening and requires urgent medical intervention and hospitalization.

## Case Study 4 – Serious and Unexpected

A patient develops a previously unlisted serious adverse reaction that results in hospitalization. The reaction is not described in the applicable product information.

**Severity:** Severe

**Seriousness:** Serious

**Expectedness:** Unexpected

**Clinical impact:** Significant; hospitalization and an unexpected reaction require careful pharmacovigilance evaluation.

## Case Study 5 – Severe but Non-serious

A patient experiences severe headache that causes substantial discomfort but does not require hospitalization, is not life-threatening, and does not result in disability or another serious outcome.

**Severity:** Severe

**Seriousness:** Non-serious

**Expectedness:** Determined from the applicable product information.

**Clinical impact:** High symptom intensity, but the case does not automatically meet regulatory seriousness criteria.

| Case 3 | Severe | Serious | Depends on reference information | Significant |

## Factors Considered

1. Intensity of the reaction.
2. Patient outcome.
3. Need for hospitalization.
4. Life-threatening potential.
5. Disability or incapacity.
6. Congenital anomaly or birth defect.
7. Need for important medical intervention.
8. Whether the reaction is listed in applicable product information.
9. Relevant clinical history and alternative causes.

## Conclusion

Severity, seriousness, and expectedness are separate elements of pharmacovigilance case assessment. A severe reaction is not necessarily serious, and a serious reaction is determined by defined clinical or outcome criteria. Correct classification helps ensure appropriate evaluation, documentation, follow-up, and reporting of safety cases.
